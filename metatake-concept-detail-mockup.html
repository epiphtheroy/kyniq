The idea

What "repetition compulsion" means

Freud named it in Beyond the Pleasure Principle (1920): the disquieting observation that we do not only seek pleasure and avoid pain — we are driven, against our own interest, to repeat the very experiences that wounded us. The shell-shocked soldier returns to the trench in his dreams; the child who lost a mother stages the disappearance again and again with a wooden reel (the famous fort-da); the analytic patient re-enacts an old betrayal rather than remembering it. Repetition, Freud argued, is the psyche's attempt to master after the fact a trauma it could not absorb the first time — and behind that compulsion he heard, for the first time, the death drive: a pull beyond the pleasure principle, toward the inorganic stillness from which life departed.

Cinema is built for this. A film is itself a loop that can be run again, a return engineered into the medium — so it is no accident that the screen overflows with characters who circle back: the detective who must remake a dead woman, the survivor who restages the disaster, the loop that resets until the wound is named. metatake gathers 248 films in which a critic has read exactly this movement — the unmastered wound staged once more instead of mourned — and maps them onto the figures, tropes and theorists that carry the idea. The theorist

Whose idea this is — the figure metatake credits as the concept's source
SF
Theorist · Psychoanalysis
Sigmund Freud
1856–1939 · Vienna · founder of psychoanalysis
Freud introduced the repetition compulsion in Beyond the Pleasure Principle (1920), where the puzzle of why traumatised minds return to their wounds — and why play, dreams and transference are structured by return — led him to posit a force "beyond" pleasure. From that compulsion he drew the concept's lineage to the death drive (Todestrieb): the pull toward the inanimate that shadows every act of living repetition.
Key text: Beyond the Pleasure Principle (1920) · bio note illustrative — to be sourced via the Combined Intellectuals database.
