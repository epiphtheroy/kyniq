The reading

What this trope claims

A dense Levinasian cluster: the trope reframes the human — or non-human — face as Levinas's visage, a silent, defenceless presence that issues the absolute command "thou shalt not kill" before a single word is exchanged. The summons is not earned, deserved, or negotiated; it is conferred by the encounter itself. Across cinema the figure recurs whenever a held close-up obligates the viewer ethically rather than psychologically — when the screen stops asking us to understand a character and starts demanding that we answer for them.

Read this way, the face of the Other summons responsibility before reason, contract, or consent. Nearly every member reading turns on an obligation that the self did not choose: the donkey that cannot speak, the stranger's child, the refugee on the doorstep, the dying parent, the camera that turns away. The trope spans 61 films and three argued frameworks — and the same demand is honored in some, refused in others, and, at its darkest, annihilated. The films do not illustrate the idea; they each independently arrive at it.
