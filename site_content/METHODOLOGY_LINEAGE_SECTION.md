## Lineage — the awards, canons and national record

Alongside the readings, Metatake keeps a second, plainer kind of knowledge about each film: where it sits in cinema's public record. **Lineage** is a separate structured layer — not embeddings, not close readings — that files a film against the traditions it belongs to. It tracks **national cinemas** (41), **film movements** (34), **awards** (55), **canons** (15), and **auteur lines** (160). Across roughly 115 populated award, canon and national lists, it holds **10,000+ film-to-list memberships** spanning about **6,700 distinct films**. Where a reading tells you what a film is thinking about, lineage tells you what company it keeps.

The method is **complete enumeration, not sampling**. When we add the Palme d'Or we add *every* Palme d'Or; when we add the Academy Award for Best Picture we take the whole winner history; the TSPDT 1000 Greatest Films goes in as all 1,000, in order. Ranked canons — Sight & Sound, the AFI lists, the BBC critics' polls, the US National Film Registry — go in whole, not as a hand-picked highlight reel. A list is either the real thing end to end or it isn't on the site.

Every entry names where it came from. Lists are **compiled from official records** — award bodies and festival archives — **and from Wikipedia/Wikidata**, cross-read against the canon-owners' own published lists, and each entry on the site cites the specific list and source it was drawn from. You can see, per film, which award or canon put it there and where that record lives.

Once compiled, every entry is **resolved to a film's TMDb identity**, the same identity the rest of the site is built on, so a lineage membership and a close reading point at the same film. Where a title can't be matched with confidence, it is **held rather than guessed** — so a list may honestly read **"N of M" resolved** instead of pretending to be complete. A short count is a real count, not a rounded one.

Verification runs before anything shows. Every row is **structurally validated**; each list's size is **checked against its known real length**, so a poll that should have fifty entries can't quietly ship with forty. Partial lists are **labelled partial** rather than dressed up as whole. And a few subjective national critics' polls, for which no clean public source exists, are **held back — hidden — pending verification** rather than shown as authoritative. Lineage sits under the same [corrections](https://metatake.net/methodology#corrections) loop as everything else: if a film is filed under the wrong award or missing from a canon it belongs to, tell us and we'll fix the row.

Because lineage is structured, it reads both ways. Follow a film out to its traditions — or read a set of films *back* into them. The pages you've spent time with can be resolved into the lineages they've travelled: which national cinemas, which movements, which awards and canons your films actually sit in. It's a way of seeing the shape of a viewing habit in cinema's own terms, computed from the record, not asserted.

A word on limits. These are **winners and ranked members, not full nominee slates** — a Best Picture list holds the film that won, not the four it beat. A handful of lists are partial and say so. And box office and audience ratings aren't kept here; those live where they belong, on [TMDb](https://www.themoviedb.org/), not in the lineage layer.

## 4. Honesty note — provenance phrasing

Anywhere the site currently says a list was **"compiled from Wikidata"**, replace it with the more accurate and more trustworthy:

> **"compiled from official records and Wikipedia/Wikidata, resolved to TMDb."**

The lists are not scraped from a single database. They are assembled from official award and festival records and from Wikipedia/Wikidata, checked against the canon-owners' published lists, and then matched to TMDb identities. The longer phrase is not marketing — it is what actually happened, and it is the version a reader can check.
