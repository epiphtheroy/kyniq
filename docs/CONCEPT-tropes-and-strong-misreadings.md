## Strong Misreadings — manifesto (verbatim, for About page)

**Strong Misreadings**
_Powerful readings, offered as exactly that — and no more._

Every interpretation in this section arrives under a name that admits its own risk.

The critic Harold Bloom argued that no reading is innocent or neutral — that "reading is always misreading," and that the readings worth having are the strong ones: forceful, willful interpretations that wrest a new meaning from a work instead of dutifully recovering its obvious sense. A strong misreading earns its keep not by being correct, but by how much it lets you see.

That is the wager here. Each Strong Misreading takes a surface detail — an image, a line, a fact about how the film was made — distrusts it, and turns it into something the film never says aloud. The lineage is old and respectable: Nietzsche's claim that there are no facts, only interpretations; Ricoeur's "hermeneutics of suspicion," which read Marx, Nietzsche and Freud as masters of distrust; Jonathan Culler's open defense of overinterpretation, on the argument that the cautious, moderate reading tends to recover only what we already knew. Richard Rorty put it bluntly: don't ask what a text really means — ask what you can do with it.

Against all of them stands Umberto Eco, who spent a career warning about the reader who connects everything and finds a secret behind every detail. We take the warning seriously. We are, knowingly, close to the kind of reading he distrusted.

Which is why the name is what it is. To call these Strong Misreadings is to keep a promise: we will push a reading as far as it will go, and we will not pretend the result is the truth of the film. "Misreading" is the disclaimer built into the title. A reading this bold forfeits any right to also call itself the correct one — so it doesn't. Each is a long shot taken on purpose, one swerve away from the obvious, offered for what it opens up rather than what it settles.

Read them as provocations, not verdicts. If one changes how a film looks to you, it has done the only thing it set out to do.

## Definitions

**Misreading** (atomic) — one bold interpretation (one film × one figure, via one of the 14 frameworks). Self-aware homage to Harold Bloom. Each is a *provocation, not a verdict*.

**Trope** (broad, redefined) — a conventional narrative/aesthetic DEVICE *or* an INTERPRETIVE PATTERN that circulates across genre and culture: "the Chosen One," "meet-cute," "Chekhov's gun." We recognize it because we have met it in other works. Meaning runs **top-down** — from the shared convention into the single film. A trope is **intertextual, a priori, shared property**. *It need not be a set of figures* — it is any recognizable recurring pattern, whether of shape OR of reading.

## The unifying insight (the site's signature idea)
A **Strong Misreading is a trope-in-the-making.** The instant a bold reading recurs across films, it stops being singular and becomes a recurring *interpretive* pattern — i.e. a trope. So every pattern on the site sits somewhere on a **maturity arc**, read from **recurrence (count)** and **cohesion (embedding tightness)**:

- **Noble** — singular (n=1). A misreading so original it stands alone; not yet shared. The boldest, most singular thing the site holds.
- **Fresh / Emerging** — rare, tight recurrence (small n). A pattern just *beginning* to be shared — a genuine discovery ("only these films, in all of cinema, do this").
- **Established** — a real recurring pattern across many films.
- **Cliché / Convention** — fully conventional, culturally given, top-down ("Chekhov's gun"). The most shared, the least singular.

Two origins feed the same arc:
- **Bottom-up** — clusters of the site's own Strong Misreadings → land at the *Noble / Fresh* end.
- **Top-down** — recognized cultural conventions → sit at the *Cliché* end.

The site's job is to **map where every device and every reading sits on this arc** — to catch a misreading at the noble moment, and to name a cliché for what it has become.

## Structure (no "meta-take" layer)
- **Atomic units**: Figures (concrete things a film returns to) + Strong Misreadings (bold readings of figures).
- **Trope** = a recurring pattern. Members are figure/film instances (for *convention/device* tropes) and/or recurring Strong Misreadings (for *misreading* tropes). Attributes:
  - **name** — dramatic, curiosity-provoking, ≤8 words. Entering should feel like "an uncannily perfect set has been gathered here."
  - **kind** — `convention` (device/shape) vs `misreading` (clustered Strong Misreadings) — marked differently in UI.
  - **maturity** — Noble → Fresh → Established → Cliché (from count + cohesion).
  - **similar tropes** — many, by embedding. *This replaces any rollup; tropes are not gathered into meta-takes.*
- A **figure ∈ many tropes**. A figure can also appear in no trope (singular = noble).
- The **old take text** = the figure's *description within a trope* (how this figure instantiates it).
- **Minimum trope size = 2** (a true pair across all of cinema is already a discovery). Singular misreadings are shown as **Noble**, not as multi-member tropes.

## What makes a trope — the STRIKINGNESS filter (not frequency)
A trope is a **semiotically loaded code, not a repeated attribute.** (Thompson: a motif is "the smallest element of a tale with the power to persist in tradition," and its condition is **strikingness** — "the mother is not a motif; the cruel stepmother is.")

- **Attribute = frequency. Trope = function.** "A tall character" is a countable statistical regularity — *repetition, not a trope.* Height becomes a trope only when it is **bundled with meaning + expectation** and read as a code: *the Gentle Giant* (size + unexpected tenderness, subverting the threat), *tall-dark-and-handsome*, *the towering villain* (height as shorthand for menace). What sediments into tradition is **coded repetition**; a bare attribute never crystallizes, however frequent.
- **Consequence for clustering**: embedding proximity only **proposes candidates** — it will happily group bare attributes ("tall people," "red objects," "rain"). The **critic (LLM) is the arbiter**: keep a candidate only if it carries a code (a meaning + an activated expectation); discard bare-attribute / incoherent groups; give each an **evocative ≤8-word name that states the code** — *"The Gentle Giant," never "Tall Characters."* This is criticism, not classification.
- **Consequence for the figure DB**: the unit-selection filter is **strikingness + semiotic loading, not frequency** — Thompson's mother-vs-stepmother test is the gem filter across ~200k figures.
