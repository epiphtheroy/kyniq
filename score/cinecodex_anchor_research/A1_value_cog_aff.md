## COG — Cognitive Yield
*Definition: New ways of thinking or perceiving that persist after viewing. 0 = nothing new → 100 = lasting conceptual/perceptual shift.*

---

### COG 0 — No cognitive yield

Films where entertainment is purely kinetic/visceral; viewers leave with zero new frameworks.

| Film | Reason (≤12 words) |
|---|---|
| Transformers: Age of Extinction (2014, Michael Bay) | Pure spectacle; no idea survives the credits. |
| Fast & Furious 7 (2015, James Wan) | Plot is pretext; thinking is actively discouraged. |
| Resident Evil: Afterlife (2010, Paul W.S. Anderson) | Game-logic action loop with zero conceptual payload. |
| Baywatch (2017, Seth Gordon) | Franchise brand exercise; intellect not invited. |
| Jack and Jill (2011, Dennis Dugan) | Gag anthology; no residue of thought. |

*Note: These are not moral verdicts — some are pleasurable. They anchor the floor of COG.*

---

### COG 25 — Mild cognitive yield

Films with a clear thesis or one interesting idea, delivered conventionally; viewers leave mildly enriched.

| Film | Reason (≤12 words) |
|---|---|
| Parasite (2019, Bong Joon-ho) | Class-war thesis sharp but legible; wears themes openly. |
| Get Out (2017, Jordan Peele) | One strong racial-liberalism inversion; not structurally destabilizing. |
| The Social Network (2010, David Fincher) | Asks what ambition costs; answers remain surface-level. |
| Arrival (2016, Denis Villeneuve) | Sapir-Whorf hook is genuine; resolved too neatly. |
| Incendies (2010, Denis Villeneuve) | Structural reveal reframes war; emotional > cognitive payload. |
| Joker (2019, Todd Phillips) | Gestures at societal failure; thesis too easily consumed. |

---

### COG 50 — Real ideas, conventionally delivered

Films carrying substantive intellectual content — philosophy, structural argument, perceptual challenge — but framed accessibly enough that the ideas don't reshape the viewer's world.

| Film | Reason (≤12 words) |
|---|---|
| Rashomon (1950, Akira Kurosawa) | Demonstrates perspectivism via structure; idea clear, delivery classical. |
| Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind (2004, Michel Gondry) | Memory-and-identity thesis real but emotionally packaged for mass consumption. |
| Mulholland Drive (2001, David Lynch) | Dream-logic puzzle; cognitive demand high, but decipherable over time. |
| Yi Yi (2000, Edward Yang) | Perceptions-of-life meditation structurally embedded; accessible to patient viewer. |
| The Act of Killing (2012, Joshua Oppenheimer) | Perpetrator-performance frame reframes trauma testimony; jarring but graspable. |
| Caché (2005, Michael Haneke) | Surveillance and colonial guilt as film grammar; legible with effort. |

---

### COG 75 — Reorganizes how you see something

Viewers report genuinely altered frameworks after watching — about time, labor, bodies, cinema itself.

| Film | Reason (≤12 words) |
|---|---|
| Jeanne Dielman, 23 Quai du Commerce (1975, Chantal Akerman) | Real-time domestic labor rewires perception of women's invisible work. |
| Sans Soleil (1983, Chris Marker) | Reframes memory, recording, and the politics of looking. |
| Certified Copy (2010, Abbas Kiarostami) | Dissolves authenticity/copy distinction; leaves ontology of relationships permanently unstable. |
| Funny Games (1997, Michael Haneke) | Forces audience to confront their own complicity in screen violence. |
| The Mirror (1975, Andrei Tarkovsky) | Autobiographical/historical montage rewires how we process memory and time. |
| Close-Up (1990, Abbas Kiarostami) | Fiction/document collapse; viewer can never unsee the frame again. |

Sources: [Sight & Sound on Jeanne Dielman](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/keeping-distance-chantal-akerman-jeanne-dielman) · [Rhizomes on Sans Soleil memory](https://rhizomes.net/issue8/tryon.htm) · [Offscreen on Certified Copy](https://offscreen.com/view/platonism-in-abbas-kiarostamias-certified-copy) · [Criterion on Funny Games](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/6347-funny-games-don-t-you-want-to-see-how-it-ends)

---

### COG 100 — Lasting conceptual/perceptual shift

Watching permanently alters how the viewer thinks about consciousness, time, cinema, or the human condition. Consensus across critical traditions.

| Film | Reason (≤12 words) |
|---|---|
| 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968, Stanley Kubrick) | Rewires sense of cosmic scale; evolution and tools as ontology. |
| Stalker (1979, Andrei Tarkovsky) | Faith, desire, and the Zone restructure how viewer inhabits uncertainty. |
| Satantango (1994, Béla Tarr) | Seven-hour durational form; cynicism and communal delusion become embodied knowledge. |
| Werckmeister Harmonies (2000, Béla Tarr) | Collapse of order made physically felt; political allegory as perception. |
| The Tree of Life (2011, Terrence Malick) | Grief and cosmology fused; transforms how viewer locates self in time. |
| Shoah (1985, Claude Lanzmann) | Nine hours of testimony; alters forever how witness and history relate. |

Sources: [Senses of Cinema on 2001](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2009/feature-articles/stanley-kubricks-2001-an-existential-odyssey/) · [Criterion on Werckmeister](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/8442-werckmeister-harmonies-dark-side-of-the-earth) · [Shoah on Criterion](https://www.criterion.com/films/27968-shoah) · [Psychology Today on Tree of Life](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/evil-deeds/201107/the-tree-life-does-terrence-malicks-new-film-bear-artistic-or-philosophical)

## AFF — Affective Yield
*Definition: DURABLE emotional/aesthetic resonance that persists after viewing. Not momentary thrill. 0 = forgettable → 100 = indelible imprint. Accessibility/popularity is NOT a cap.*

---

### AFF 0 — No affective residue

Films that produce in-theatre sensation (excitement, laughs) but leave no emotional trace within 48 hours.

| Film | Reason (≤12 words) |
|---|---|
| Transformers: Dark of the Moon (2011, Michael Bay) | Sensation during; nothing lingers. Emotion designed to expire. |
| Pixels (2015, Chris Columbus) | Nostalgia pastiche; feelings are borrowed and immediately returned. |
| The Mummy (2017, Alex Kurtzman) | Brand-launch anxiety with no emotional stakes for any character. |
| Holmes & Watson (2018, Etan Cohen) | Even the laughter is quickly forgotten. |
| Emoji Movie (2017, Tony Leondis) | Brand-assembly product; no emotional character investment possible. |

---

### AFF 25 — Pleasant then gone

Warm or exciting in the moment; one or two lingering beats but nothing returns to the viewer unprompted.

| Film | Reason (≤12 words) |
|---|---|
| Avengers: Endgame (2019, Russo Brothers) | Portals moment briefly sublime; emotional logic dissolves post-catharsis. |
| La La Land (2016, Damien Chazelle) | Final sequence genuinely stings; then the affect dissipates cleanly. |
| Amélie (2001, Jean-Pierre Jeunet) | Delightful texture; warmth fades with the whimsy. |
| Crazy Rich Asians (2018, Jon M. Chu) | Genuine crowd-pleasure; emotional stakes mild and self-resolved. |
| Midnight in Paris (2011, Woody Allen) | Gentle nostalgia hit; no emotional wound to return to. |

---

### AFF 50 — A few lingering moments

Scenes, images, or performances persist for days or weeks; the film as a whole doesn't haunt but specific moments resurface.

| Film | Reason (≤12 words) |
|---|---|
| Spirited Away (2001, Hayao Miyazaki) | Train-over-water scene returns; overall structure doesn't haunt. |
| City of God (2002, Fernando Meirelles) | Runts gang's end lingers; kinetic pace dilutes sustained resonance. |
| Princess Mononoke (1997, Hayao Miyazaki) | Forest imagery and final confrontation linger; emotional arc mostly resolved. |
| Bicycle Thieves (1948, Vittorio De Sica) | Final father-son scene returns reliably; neorealist clarity limits haunting. |
| Her (2013, Spike Jonze) | Loneliness metaphor lingers; emotional resolution too clean for 75. |
| The Farewell (2019, Lulu Wang) | Deathbed comedy lands; affect warm but not searing. |

---

### AFF 75 — Returns to you for weeks

The film arrives unbidden in the viewer's mind over days and weeks — images, scenes, or the overall emotional register intrudes involuntarily.

| Film | Reason (≤12 words) |
|---|---|
| In the Mood for Love (2000, Wong Kar-wai) | Unrequited longing returns in slow-motion; the music haunts for weeks. |
| Manchester by the Sea (2016, Kenneth Lonergan) | Grief without catharsis; Affleck's wall returns unexpectedly for months. |
| Roma (2018, Alfonso Cuarón) | Ocean birth-scream scene arrives unprompted; personal memory activated. |
| Come and See (1985, Elem Klimov) | War trauma imprinted at a cellular level; face haunts for weeks. |
| Yi Yi (2000, Edward Yang) | Ting-Ting's final monologue returns; everyday life suddenly charged. |
| A Separation (2011, Asghar Farhadi) | Moral impasse unresolved; viewer keeps re-arguing the case. |

Sources: [BFI on Wong Kar-wai emotion](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/mood-love-wong-kar-wai-emotion) · [Film Quarterly on Roma affect](https://filmquarterly.org/2019/06/07/roma-silence-language-and-the-ambiguous-power-of-affect/)

---

### AFF 100 — Indelible imprint

Viewing leaves a permanent emotional-aesthetic mark; viewers describe themselves as changed people. Critical consensus across decades and cultures.

| Film | Reason (≤12 words) |
|---|---|
| Grave of the Fireflies (1988, Isao Takahata) | War's cost on children; sorrow doesn't fade across decades. |
| Shoah (1985, Claude Lanzmann) | Testimony accumulates to unbearable weight; the faces never leave. |
| Au Hasard Balthazar (1966, Robert Bresson) | Donkey's suffering as grace; imprints a specific form of sorrow. |
| Dancer in the Dark (2000, Lars von Trier) | Bjork's execution: a wound that reopens on recall. |
| Andrei Rublev (1966, Andrei Tarkovsky) | Bell-casting sequence and final color burst — permanent aesthetic imprinting. |
| Son of Saul (2015, László Nemes) | Narrow depth-of-field traps viewer inside Auschwitz; horror becomes bodily. |

Sources: [IDA on Shoah emotional journey](https://www.documentary.org/online-feature/adam-benzine-tracking-claude-lanzmanns-emotional-journey-make-shoah) · [neurolaunch.com on lasting emotional impact](https://neurolaunch.com/emotional-movies/)

## 고민/결정 노트

- **COG와 AFF는 독립 변수다.** Shoah가 COG 100과 AFF 100에 동시에 배치된 것은 의도적이다. 두 차원 모두 최고 밴드를 정당화할 수 있는 증거가 독립적으로 존재한다. 한 차원의 점수가 다른 차원을 자동 결정하지 않는다는 원칙을 실증하는 사례다.

- **Parasite COG 25 배치 논쟁 가능성.** 많은 시네필이 Parasite를 높이 평가하지만, 계급 비판의 '테제'가 너무 명시적으로 제시된다 — 관객이 새로운 프레임을 스스로 구성할 여지가 적다. 이것이 COG 25에 놓은 이유다. Funny Games(COG 75)와 비교: Haneke는 관객이 무엇을 보고 있는지 스스로 발견하게 강제한다.

- **Spirited Away/Princess Mononoke AFF 50 배치.** 두 작품 모두 '명작 애니메이션'으로 분류되지만, 정서적 지속성 기준으로 보면 개별 장면이 기억에 남지 전체가 '주 단위로 되돌아오는' 수준은 아니다. Ghibli 팬덤이 이의를 제기할 수 있으나, Come and See나 Manchester by the Sea와 비교하면 지속적 침투력에서 차이가 명확하다.

- **COG 75 vs COG 100의 경계선.** 기준은 '재구성(reorganize)'과 '전이(transform)'의 차이다. Certified Copy(COG 75)는 진위/복사의 이분법을 흔들지만, 2001이나 Stalker처럼 관객의 우주론적 자기인식 자체를 바꾸지는 않는다. 이 경계는 관객 경험의 '되돌릴 수 없음(irreversibility)'을 기준으로 판단했다.

- **Dancer in the Dark AFF 100 배치 — 접근성과 무관.** 폰 트리어 작품은 종종 '도발'로 분류되어 정서적 가치가 과소평가된다. 그러나 Bjork의 처형 시퀀스는 수년이 지나도 관객에게 신체적 수준의 반응을 유발한다는 증거가 충분하다. 인기와 접근성이 AFF의 상한이 아니라는 원칙을 여기서 실천했다.

- **세계 영화 편향 교정.** 초안에서 서유럽·일본 편중이 있었다. 최종본에서 Farhadi(이란), Meirelles(브라질), Lanzmann(유럽 유대 역사), Klimov(소련), Nemes(헝가리), Cuarón(멕시코), Yang(대만)을 의식적으로 포함했다. 그러나 아프리카, 남아시아 영화가 여전히 없는 것은 한계이며 Layer B 검토에서 보완 권고.
