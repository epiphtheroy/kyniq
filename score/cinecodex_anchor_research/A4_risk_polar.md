# A4 — RISK / POLAR (Polarization) Anchor Research

**Sub-dimension:** POLAR (0–100, higher = more divisive)
**Definition used:** How sharply INFORMED, ENGAGED viewers split on a film. POLAR measures *real disagreement among people who actually watched and engaged* — NOT thin data, niche appeal, or low view counts.
**Primary dispersion proxies (in priority order):**
1. Rotten Tomatoes **Tomatometer (critic) vs Popcornmeter (audience) gap** — large gaps signal a critic/audience divide.
2. Metacritic **Metascore vs User Score gap**.
3. **Letterboxd / MovieLens rating standard deviation** (the cleanest single-population dispersion measure; bimodal/flat histograms).
4. **Festival reception** (Cannes simultaneous boos + standing ovations = the purest real-time love/hate signal).
5. Forum/critical discourse evidencing a love-it-or-hate-it split.

All scores below were read directly off the cited pages (Rotten Tomatoes pages fetched June 2026; standard-deviation claims from Letterboxd/StatSignificant analyses). Where a gap is given it is `critic% − audience%` on Rotten Tomatoes unless noted.

## POLAR

### Band 0–15 — Near-consensus (almost everyone agrees)

These are anchors for the *bottom* of the scale: informed viewers overwhelmingly converge. Low critic↔audience gap AND high agreement on both sides.

- **Parasite** (2019, Bong Joon-ho) — RT 99% critic / 90% audience; only 9-pt gap, both near-ceiling. [RT]
- **Spirited Away** (2001, Miyazaki) — RT 96–97% critic with comparably high audience; broad cross-demographic consensus, no organized backlash. [RT/WatchMojo]
- **Seven Samurai** (1954, Kurosawa) — RT 100% critic; canonical near-unanimity among engaged viewers. [RT/WatchMojo]
- **Toy Story** (1995, Lasseter) — RT 100% critic with very high audience; rare zero-detractor family/critical overlap. [RT/WatchMojo]
- **The Tree of Life** audience-favorable counterpart note: NOT here (see 50–65). Use instead **Toy Story / Parasite** as clean low-POLAR anchors.

*Rationale:* near-consensus is the absence of a vocal informed faction arguing the film is bad. These films have essentially no "love-it-or-hate-it" camp among people who engaged.

### Band 25–45 — Mild (strong consensus WITH a vocal minority backlash)

Dominant critical consensus, but a real, articulate dissenting minority exists.

- **Synecdoche, New York** (2008, Kaufman) — RT 69% critic / 71% audience (gap only ~2 pts), but reviews split hard "masterpiece" vs "pretentious/incomprehensible"; Ebert's #1 of the decade vs a sizable "self-indulgent" camp. The near-equal aggregate *masks* a vocal-minority split → mild. [RT]
- **The Master** (2012, P.T. Anderson) — Metacritic 86 (universal acclaim) but a persistent "cold/shut-out/underwritten" dissent; Venice Silver Lion alongside "slams the door in your face" reviews. [Wikipedia/Metacritic]
- **Beau Is Afraid** (2023, Ari Aster) — RT 74% critic / 72% audience (gap ~2 pts) yet discourse explicitly "some loved it, others hated it"; box-office bomb despite acclaim → engaged-viewer backlash without an aggregate gap. [RT/Screen Rant]
- **The Brutalist** (2024, Brady Corbet) — RT 94% critic / 69% audience (25-pt gap); critics' "masterpiece" vs Letterboxd "overhyped / 215-min slog" minority. [RT/Screen Rant]
- **A Clockwork Orange** (1971, Kubrick) — flagged by StatSignificant as a transgressive title with enduring dual-legacy reception (adoration + moral-decay dismissal). [StatSignificant]

### Band 50–65 — Notably divisive

A genuine, sizable critic↔audience or within-population gap; clearly contested but not a clean 50/50 split.

- **The Tree of Life** (2011, Malick) — RT 86% critic / **60% audience (26-pt gap)**; Cannes premiere drew *both boos and applause* before winning the Palme d'Or; audience reviews openly split "mind-expanding pure art" vs "painfully pretentious." [RT/Wikipedia/RT Editorial]
- **mother!** (2017, Aronofsky) — RT 68% critic / **51% audience (17-pt gap)** plus a rare **CinemaScore "F"**; critic "singular vision" vs audience "worst movie I've ever seen." The F-grade-with-fresh-critics combo is a textbook divide. [RT/Hollywood Reporter]
- **Spring Breakers** (2013, Korine) — RT 67% critic / **38% audience (29-pt gap)**; "stinging satire" (critics) vs "shallow, hollow waste" (audiences). [RT]
- **Twilight** (2008, Hardwicke) — named in StatSignificant's high-variance set: "loved with fervent devotion or hated for its sheer cultural pervasiveness." [StatSignificant]
- **Moulin Rouge!** (2001, Luhrmann) — on Letterboxd's most-divisive (≥10,000 ratings) list by standard-deviation; flagged as a film with a strong fan base and an equally strong detractor base. [Letterboxd Journal]

### Band 70–100 — Genuine half-acclaim / half-dismissal (love-it-or-hate-it)

The purest splits: simultaneous boos AND ovations, near-50/50 aggregate scores, or top-of-list rating standard deviation among films with *large* engaged samples.

- **Star Wars: The Last Jedi** (2017, Rian Johnson) — RT 91% critic / **41% audience — a ~50-point gap**, "one of the biggest critic-vs-audience splits ever" (Information Is Beautiful); 100,000+ ratings, so unmistakably engaged-viewer division, not thin data. [RT/Information Is Beautiful]
- **Antichrist** (2009, von Trier) — RT **53% critic / 55% audience** (both straddle 50 = maximal disagreement *within* each group); Cannes 2009 premiere had ~4 faintings, walkouts, boos AND a Best Actress win; ecumenical jury "anti-award." [RT/Wikipedia]
- **Only God Forgives** (2013, Refn) — RT 41% critic / 37% audience; Cannes 2013 ending where **"half the audience booed and the other half rose cheering"** — the literal love/hate snapshot. [RT/Wikipedia]
- **Megalopolis** (2024, Coppola) — RT ~50% critic; Cannes 2024 drew **boos then a 7–10-minute standing ovation**; reviews ranged "insufferable mess" to "beautiful nightmare," explicitly "love it or hate it." [RT/MovieWeb/Hollywood Reporter]
- **The Room** (2003, Wiseau) — tops Letterboxd's **most-divisive list (≥10,000 ratings) by rating standard deviation**; perennial audience-splitter. [Letterboxd Journal]
- **The Idiots** (1998, von Trier) — Cannes screening so divisive critic Mark Kermode was ejected screaming "It is shit"; cited by StatSignificant as the archetypal class-splitting film. [StatSignificant]

### Band 16–24 / supplementary — Low-mild boundary anchors

(Bridge anchors so raters can calibrate the gap between near-consensus and mild.)

- **Plan 9 from Outer Space** (1959, Ed Wood) — **#1 most polarizing in StatSignificant's MovieLens standard-deviation ranking**; "inept disaster" vs "ineptitude adds to its charm." Note: this is divisive among the *cult-engaged*, edges toward the niche caveat — use as an illustration of the divisive-vs-niche boundary, not a pure high-POLAR anchor. [StatSignificant]
- **Pink Flamingos** (1972, Waters) — high MovieLens variance; transgression that delights some / repels others. [StatSignificant]

## 고민/결정 노트 (Reasoning & Decisions)

### How I distinguished DIVISIVE from NICHE / thin data
This is the load-bearing distinction the task demands, and I enforced it three ways:

1. **Sample-size floor on dispersion claims.** A high rating standard deviation only counts as POLAR if it comes from a *large engaged sample*. That's why I leaned on Letterboxd's "≥10,000 ratings" divisive list rather than its "≥500 ratings" list — the latter is topped by *Cool Cat Saves the Kids*, which is "divisive" only because almost nobody saw it and the handful who did split. That is thin data masquerading as polarization, and I deliberately excluded it from the high bands. *The Room* (also Wiseau-tier "so-bad-it's-good") DOES qualify because it has a massive, genuinely split engaged audience.

2. **Two-population cross-check (critic gap + within-population spread).** The cleanest high-POLAR signal is when *both* populations sit near 50% (Antichrist 53/55, Only God Forgives 41/37) — that means disagreement *inside* each group, not just critics-vs-crowd. The next cleanest is a huge cross-population gap on a large sample (Last Jedi 91/41 over 100k ratings). I trust these more than a lopsided "low on both" panning, which is consensus-negative, not polarized. I noted this caveat explicitly on Only God Forgives (its 41/37 is partly "panned-with-defenders," rescued into the band by the literal half-booed/half-cheered Cannes screening).

3. **Festival real-time signal as the gold standard.** Simultaneous boos AND standing ovations at the same Cannes screening (Tree of Life, Megalopolis, Only God Forgives, Antichrist) is the purest possible evidence that engaged, informed viewers split in real time — impossible to confuse with niche thinness, since these are packed premieres of high-profile films.

### Why some near-equal-score films are only MILD, not low
Synecdoche (69/71) and Beau Is Afraid (74/72) have almost no aggregate gap, yet I placed them at MILD (25–45), not near-consensus. Reason: aggregate parity can *hide* a bimodal split — a film where half rate 5★ and half rate 1★ averages out the same as a film everyone rates 3★. The qualitative discourse ("masterpiece" vs "pretentious"; "loved it / hated it") plus Beau's box-office collapse despite acclaim are the tells. This is exactly the case where the percent-gap proxy fails and you must read the distribution shape, not the mean.

### Data limitations
- **RT Popcornmeter changed methodology** (Verified/"Hot" ratings since 2024), so older audience percentages may not be strictly comparable across eras; I used current on-page values and dated the fetch (June 2026).
- **No public per-film Letterboxd standard-deviation numbers.** Letterboxd publishes the *ranked divisive lists* but not the raw σ per title, so for individual films I triangulate σ-rank membership + RT gap + discourse rather than citing a single σ figure. The only clean σ-method source with named films is StatSignificant (MovieLens, 1995–2015 — so it under-weights post-2015 films like mother!, Last Jedi, Beau, Megalopolis, which is why those rely on RT gaps + festival evidence instead).
- **CinemaScore is opening-night-only and US-only**, so the mother! "F" reflects first-day paying audiences, not all engaged viewers — but combined with the 68/51 RT split it's corroborating, not sole, evidence.
- **Banding is ordinal, not metric.** A 26-pt RT gap (Tree of Life) and a 29-pt gap (Spring Breakers) both sit in 50–65 because gap size alone doesn't linearly map to POLAR; the 70–100 band is reserved for *near-50/50 or festival-confirmed* splits, which is a qualitatively stronger signal than any single gap number.
- **Selection toward cinephile catalog:** I prioritized divisive auteur/festival titles (von Trier, Noé-adjacent, Aronofsky, Korine, Malick, Kaufman, Coppola, Refn, Corbet) per the task, which means the bands skew arthouse; a mainstream-heavy catalog would also need franchise-backlash anchors (Transformers, Twilight, Last Jedi) — Last Jedi and Twilight are included as bridges.
