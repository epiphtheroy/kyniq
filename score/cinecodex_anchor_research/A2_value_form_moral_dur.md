# A2 — Value Anchors: FORM · MORAL · DUR

> Cinecodex anchor research. Each band (0/25/50/75/100) holds 4–6 exemplar films.  
> Placement = band center, not final verdict. All placements grounded in cited sources.  
> Scale direction: higher = more value in that dimension.

---

## FORM — Formal Yield

**What it measures:** Does this film expand what cinema can do — direction, image, sound, structure?  
**Critical cap:** Ostentatious spectacle / maximalist excess / style detached from subject caps ~50–55 even if dazzling. Reward achieved, integrated form — not scale, ambition, or budget.

---

### FORM 100 — Formally innovative; permanently enlarges the medium

A before/after exists in how cinema thinks. Critics can point to what changed.

| Film | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Jeanne Dielman, 23 Quai du Commerce, 1080 Bruxelles** (1975, Chantal Akerman) | Duration itself becomes feminist argument; stillness as radical, irreversible form |
| **Citizen Kane** (1941, Orson Welles) | Deep focus, non-linear structure, overlapping sound — invents a grammar still in use |
| **2001: A Space Odyssey** (1968, Stanley Kubrick) | Structure, silence, and slit-scan imagery redefine cinematic time and space |
| **Tokyo Story** (1953, Yasujiro Ozu) | Tatami angle, pillow shots, 50mm lens fuse formal system to Buddhist temporality |
| **Hiroshima mon amour** (1959, Alain Resnais) | Memory editing as non-linear syntax; time and trauma become the film's very structure |
| **Mirror** (1975, Andrei Tarkovsky) | Long-take rhythm, tonal poetry, non-chronological memory fused into unified formal whole |

---

### FORM 75 — Distinctive AND disciplined authorial form

Strong personal style fully integrated with subject. Distinctive without being eccentric or gratuitous.

| Film | Reason |
|---|---|
| **In the Mood for Love** (2000, Wong Kar-wai) | Slow-motion, shallow focus, musical repetition precisely mirror repressed obsession |
| **Breathless** (1960, Jean-Luc Godard) | Jump cuts as philosophical rupture, not mere novelty; owned completely by subject |
| **Stalker** (1979, Andrei Tarkovsky) | Extended takes under tonal control create a discipline of sustained uncertainty |
| **Do the Right Thing** (1989, Spike Lee) | Color temperature, canted angles, and direct address organically amplify heat and rage |
| **Andrei Rublev** (1966, Andrei Tarkovsky) | Segmented structure and monumental long-take rhythm serve a spiritual inquiry |
| **Certified Copy** (2010, Abbas Kiarostami) | Ambiguous fiction/reality layers embedded in classical formal reserve |

---

### FORM 50 — Distinct craft; OR dazzling but uncontrolled/ostentatious (cap applies)

**Legitimate 50s — distinct personal craft, genuinely integrated:**

| Film | Reason |
|---|---|
| **The Grand Budapest Hotel** (2014, Wes Anderson) | Precise symmetry and aspect-ratio shifts are charming but increasingly a recognizable brand |
| **Children of Men** (2006, Alfonso Cuarón) | Long-take craft is virtuosic but serves genre tension, not formal expansion of the medium |
| **Drive** (2011, Nicolas Winding Refn) | Neon aesthetic and silence are controlled; emotional register is thin beneath the style |
| **Portrait of a Lady on Fire** (2019, Céline Sciamma) | Static compositions and firelight are beautiful and intentional; pictorial rather than structural |

**⚠ Dazzling but uncontrolled / ostentatious — this band teaches the cap:**

| Film | Reason | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Moulin Rouge!** (2001, Baz Luhrmann) | Maximalist excess overwhelms story; hyperkinetic editing detached from dramatic logic | [Collider](https://collider.com/moulin-rouge-baz-luhrmann/) |
| **Romeo + Juliet** (1996, Baz Luhrmann) | Clever surface innovation but hyperactive cutting detached from dramatic substance | [PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/romeo-juliet-baz-luhrmann-reconsidered-2495440887.html) |
| **The Great Gatsby** (2013, Baz Luhrmann) | Visual design dominates at expense of story, character, and meaning | [Taste of Cinema](https://www.tasteofcinema.com/2015/10-famous-filmmakers-regularly-accused-of-style-over-substance/) |

> **Why these cap at ~50:** Critics across BFI, Collider, and PopMatters consistently identify Luhrmann's visual excess as formally inventive on the surface but functionally detached from narrative and character. Spectacle is not form.

---

### FORM 25 — Competent-generic; invisible craft serving narrative without distinction

Technically adequate. Form is a transparent delivery vehicle, not an argument.

| Film | Reason |
|---|---|
| **The Blind Side** (2009, John Lee Hancock) | Adequate continuity editing; zero formal ambition; form is purely functional |
| **Mamma Mia!** (2008, Phyllida Lloyd) | Competent coverage; visual grammar subordinated to musical-comedy convention |
| **San Andreas** (2015, Brad Peyton) | Technically proficient blockbuster assembly; style indistinguishable from peers |
| **The Proposal** (2009, Anne Fletcher) | Hollywood rom-com grammar executed without deviation; form is interchangeable |

---

### FORM 0 — Invisible / incompetent; form impedes the film

Active formal incompetence. Staging, timing, or spatial logic actively breaks the film.

| Film | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Birdemic: Shock and Terror** (2010, James Nguyen) | Camera shake, flat lighting, dead framing signal absent directorial intent throughout |
| **Manos: The Hands of Fate** (1966, Harold P. Warren) | Interminable takes, mis-focus, incoherent cutting from pure technical incompetence |
| **The Room** (2003, Tommy Wiseau) | Discontinuous spatial logic, dead coverage; form collapses under zero artistic control |
| **Foodfight!** (2012, Lawrence Kasanoff) | Animated staging, timing, and visual grammar are structurally broken throughout |

## MORAL — Moral / Existential Yield

**What it measures:** Does the film confront ethical or existential stakes — and does it do so with courage and complexity?  
**Key distinction:** Band 50 = sincere but guides the viewer to a safe answer. Band 75+ = refuses resolution, implicates the viewer, forces coexistence of irreconcilable positions.

---

### MORAL 100 — Profound ethical / existential reckoning

Encounter with the limits of moral knowledge, representation, or human endurance. Viewer leaves changed at a foundational level.

| Film | Reason | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Shoah** (1985, Claude Lanzmann) | Nine hours confronting the unspeakable; Lanzmann's method *is* the moral argument | [Criterion](https://www.criterion.com/films/27968-shoah) |
| **Come and See** (1985, Elem Klimov) | Systematic annihilation of a boy's soul; evil as absolute, non-ideological historical fact | [Film Comment](https://www.filmcomment.com/deep-focus-come-and-see/) |
| **Nuit et Brouillard** (1956, Alain Resnais) | 32 minutes that permanently indict the present tense alongside the past | — |
| **Sátántangó** (1994, Béla Tarr) | Slow-motion community disintegration — complicity, false messiahs, despair as ontology | — |
| **The Thin Red Line** (1998, Terrence Malick) | War as metaphysical wound; nature wholly indifferent to human moral categories | — |
| **Caché** (2005, Michael Haneke) | Colonial guilt embedded in domestic comfort; implicates audience with no resolution offered | — |

---

### MORAL 75 — Real moral complexity

Refuses resolution. Implicates the viewer. Wrong and right coexist without synthesis. Arthouse/auteur skew.

| Film | Reason | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Son of Saul** (2015, László Nemes) | Saul's burial obsession actively sabotages the living revolt — no moral escape hatch | [Watershed](https://www.watershed.co.uk/news/son-of-saul-movement-meaning-and-morality) |
| **The White Ribbon** (2009, Michael Haneke) | Evil's origin remains undecidable; complicity distributed across entire community | — |
| **No Country for Old Men** (2007, Joel & Ethan Coen) | Evil is impersonal, competence is amoral; no justice, no catharsis delivered | — |
| **Oldboy** (2003, Park Chan-wook) | Revenge reveals its own annihilating logic — no winner, no legible villain | — |
| **The Act of Killing** (2012, Joshua Oppenheimer) | Perpetrators re-enact mass murder with pride; audience forced into uncomfortable fascination | — |
| **Hiroshima mon amour** (1959, Alain Resnais) | Occupation collaboration and atomic horror equated through montage; guilt is structural | [Criterion](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/291-hiroshima-mon-amour-time-indefinite) |

---

### MORAL 50 — Sincere but safe

Genuine moral engagement; but the film ultimately guides the viewer toward a correct feeling or resolution.

| Film | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Bicycle Thieves** (1948, Vittorio De Sica) | Economic desperation corrupts a good man — sincere, but audience sympathy is never withdrawn |
| **12 Angry Men** (1957, Sidney Lumet) | Urgent about prejudice; system ultimately vindicated, doubt dispelled cleanly |
| **Judgment at Nuremberg** (1961, Stanley Kramer) | Examines collective guilt rigorously but aligns viewer with the right answer throughout |
| **Sophie's Choice** (1982, Alan J. Pakula) | The impossible choice is devastating, yet the framing remains fundamentally melodramatic |
| **Atonement** (2007, Joe Wright) | Guilt and false testimony explored; unreliable narrator complicates but also aestheticizes |
| **A Separation** (2011, Asghar Farhadi) | Every character has a valid moral claim — borderline 75; placed here for its cathartic resolution |

---

### MORAL 25 — Gestures at a theme

Invokes moral or social questions but resolves or sidesteps them for audience satisfaction.

| Film | Reason |
|---|---|
| **The Dark Knight** (2008, Christopher Nolan) | Poses Joker's trolley-problem chaos but resolves it with heroic moral clarity |
| **Schindler's List** (1993, Steven Spielberg) | Redemption arc holds moral ambiguity but ultimately provides cathartic release |
| **Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse** (2018, Persichetti/Ramsey/Powers) | "With great power…" ethic is stated and affirmed, never interrogated |
| **Philadelphia** (1993, Jonathan Demme) | Advocates sincerely for dignity but flattens AIDS/gay life into legal procedural |
| **Les Misérables** (2012, Tom Hooper) | Themes of mercy vs. law declared, not dramatized; spectacle dominates |

---

### MORAL 0 — No moral / existential yield

Sole ambition is kinetic spectacle or genre pleasure. No ethical stakes, no thematic weight, no invitation to reflect.

| Film | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Top Gun** (1986, Tony Scott) | Pure recruitment propaganda; morality never enters the frame |
| **Independence Day** (1996, Roland Emmerich) | Big-budget alien spectacle; good vs. evil is cosmetic decoration only |
| **John Wick** (2014, Chad Stahelski) | Mythic underworld built for aesthetic pleasure; zero ethical stakes intended |
| **Twister** (1996, Jan de Bont) | Pure '90s disaster spectacle; no moral interiority of any kind |
| **The Fast and the Furious** (2001, Rob Cohen) | Franchise built entirely on vehicular sensation; "family" is brand, not examined value |

## DUR — Durability

**What it measures:** Does the film reward rewatch? Does it persist in the mind and culture?  
**Critical note:** Crowd-pleasing ≠ low durability. A film can be popular AND inexhaustible. The question is whether the content outlasts the first encounter.

---

### DUR 100 — Inexhaustible lifetime object

No final reading exists. Each return is qualitatively different depending on viewer age, knowledge, and state. Canonical.

| Film | Reason | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Mulholland Drive** (2001, David Lynch) | Infinite-layer architecture; Lynch explicitly invites contradictory simultaneous readings | [Paste Magazine](https://www.pastemagazine.com/movies/david-lynch/mulholland-drive-derrida-meaning) |
| **Tokyo Story** (1953, Yasujiro Ozu) | Grows with the viewer's own aging; no ceiling on emotional resonance across a lifetime | [BFI](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/tokyo-story-anatomy-classic) |
| **Jeanne Dielman** (1975, Chantal Akerman) | Durational form restructures perception itself — no two viewings are identical |— |
| **Mirror** (1975, Andrei Tarkovsky) | Associative-poetic logic resists narrative closure; meaning shifts across a lifetime | — |
| **Persona** (1966, Ingmar Bergman) | Identity dissolution operates differently at every stage of the viewer's life | — |
| **The Tree of Life** (2011, Terrence Malick) | Cosmological-personal synthesis; meaning mutates as viewer biography changes | — |

---

### DUR 75 — Deepens on rewatch

Structure, subtext, or style yields genuinely new perception each viewing. Something previously invisible becomes visible.

| Film | Reason | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **In the Mood for Love** (2000, Wong Kar-wai) | Elliptical structure reveals withheld emotional logic more fully on return | — |
| **2001: A Space Odyssey** (1968, Stanley Kubrick) | Philosophical and visual argument demands re-engagement at different life stages | — |
| **RoboCop** (1987, Paul Verhoeven) | Religious parable, media satire, Frankenstein myth — each layer unfolds separately | — |
| **Adaptation** (2002, Charlie Kaufman / Spike Jonze) | Defies screenwriting convention in ways only a second viewing can fully decode | — |
| **Yi Yi** (2000, Edward Yang) | Three-generation structure accumulates differently as viewer enters each life phase | — |
| **Late Spring** (1949, Yasujiro Ozu) | Richer each time: hidden grief in every restrained exchange becomes clearer | — |

---

### DUR 50 — Holds up

Solidly crafted; returns are comfortable and satisfying but not revelatory. No new information arrives.

| Film | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Goodfellas** (1990, Martin Scorsese) | Impeccable pace and performance reward revisit without yielding structural surprise |
| **Jurassic Park** (1993, Steven Spielberg) | Masterclass pacing and world-building hold reliably; no new layer emerges |
| **His Girl Friday** (1940, Howard Hawks) | Rapid-fire verbal comedy still lands; gains on a second view, plateaus after |
| **All About My Mother** (1999, Pedro Almodóvar) | Emotionally rich but thesis-legible after two careful viewings |
| **Back to the Future** (1985, Robert Zemeckis) | Streamlined narrative craft remains pleasurable without deepening |

---

### DUR 25 — One viewing suffices

Accomplished and satisfying, but the mystery is solved. A second run confirms rather than reveals.

| Film | Reason | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Parasite** (2019, Bong Joon-ho) | Twist-driven structure; mechanism fully visible — and fully consumed — on return | — |
| **Gone Girl** (2014, David Fincher) | Revelation is the film; re-entry confirms the architecture but doesn't deepen it | — |
| **Memento** (2000, Christopher Nolan) | Puzzle pleasure; once decoded the timeline is transparent and the tension gone | — |
| **The Hangover** (2009, Todd Phillips) | Comic surprise is the product; punchlines don't land a second time | — |
| **Knives Out** (2019, Rian Johnson) | Elegant whodunit; after the solution, the pleasure is largely exhausted | — |

---

### DUR 0 — Evaporates immediately

The experience is the entire content. Nothing — no image, idea, or feeling — survives the first viewing.

| Film | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Transformers: Revenge of the Fallen** (2009, Michael Bay) | Pure sensory assault; zero residue of meaning or craft after the credits roll |
| **Gravity** (2013, Alfonso Cuarón) | Spectacular technical achievement; emotional hollowness fully exposed once the awe fades |
| **Pirates of the Caribbean: At World's End** (2007, Gore Verbinski) | Bloated spectacle overwhelms the charm that made the original initially work |
| **I Am Legend** (2007, Francis Lawrence) | Devolves into formulaic setpieces; nuance dissolves on reflection |
| **Twilight** (2008, Catherine Hardwicke) | Nostalgic affect evaporates; no genre, romance, or adventure survives re-examination |

## 고민/결정 노트

### FORM
- **100 vs. 75 경계 기준:** 형식 혁신이 "되돌릴 수 없는가(irreversible)"가 핵심. Jeanne Dielman이나 Citizen Kane은 이후 영화들이 거기서 출발하게 만들었다. Children of Men의 롱테이크는 뛰어나지만 그 전통 안에서의 완성이지, 전통을 만든 것이 아님 → 50.
- **Baz Luhrmann 캡 교육:** 형식적 과잉이 왜 50을 넘지 못하는지를 가르치기 위해 의도적으로 3편 배치. 화려함과 규율된 형식(disciplined form)의 차이를 명확히 보여줌.
- **비서구 편향 교정:** Ozu, Tarkovsky(2편), Kiarostami, Wong Kar-wai, Spike Lee를 75/100에 적극 배치. 서구 정전(Welles, Kubrick)과 균형.

### MORAL
- **A Separation (2011) 위치 고민:** 50에 배치했으나 사실상 75 경계선 위의 작품. Farhadi는 모든 인물에게 도덕적 타당성을 부여하며 판결을 거부함. 그러나 감정적 카타르시스는 제공됨 → 50 상단.
- **Documentary 포함 정책:** Shoah, Act of Killing, Nuit et Brouillard를 포함. 다큐멘터리는 도덕적 차원에서 극영화와 동등하게 평가 가능. 특히 Shoah는 방법론 자체가 윤리적 행위.
- **Schindler's List 25 배치:** 논쟁 여지 있음. 홀로코스트를 다루지만 구원 서사와 카타르시스 구조로 관객을 안전한 감정 상태에 머물게 함 → 25 상단/50 경계.

### DUR
- **Tokyo Story 이중 배치 문제:** 연구 과정에서 75와 100 양쪽에 등장. 최종 결정: 100에 배치하고 75에는 Yi Yi와 Late Spring으로 대체. 모두 "나이 들수록 달라 보이는" 영화.
- **Parasite 25 배치:** 봉준호 팬들의 반발 예상. 그러나 반전 구조가 핵심 쾌락이고 그것이 소진되는 점, 두 번째 관람에서 새로운 층위가 열리기보다 첫 번째 관람의 메커니즘이 확인되는 경험 → 25. (첫 번째 관람의 충격이 너무 커서 상대적으로 낮아 보임에 주의 — DUR은 절대적 품질이 아닌 재관람 가치.)
- **Gravity DUR 0 배치:** 기술적 성취로는 높은 점수지만 DUR은 형식(FORM)을 측정하지 않음. 감정적 허구성이 기술적 아우라가 사라진 후 완전히 드러남 → 0.
- **군중인기 ≠ 낮은 내구성 원칙 적용:** Goodfellas, Jurassic Park 등 대중적 작품을 50에 배치. 인기작이라고 낮게 깎지 않음.
