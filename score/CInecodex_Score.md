**I. Immersion Index Framework**

* **A. Atmospheric Envelopment (AE):**  
  * **L1 (Detached):** Quality: Flat and artificial. No sense of spatial depth. Sets or locations feel obviously constructed. Sound is sparse, purely functional, or excessively intrusive.  
  * **L2 (Conventional):** Quality: Functional environment. The atmosphere and space are effectively established but do not overwhelm the viewer. Audiovisual elements support the mood.  
  * **L3 (Immersive):** Quality: Rich detail and texture in the mise-en-scène. Cinematography and immersive sound design maximize the depth and presence of the location, making the viewer believe they are in the environment.  
  * **L4 (Captivating):** Quality: Overwhelming presence. A total sensory experience where sight and sound combine perfectly to envelop the audience. The atmosphere becomes a dominant element.  
  * **L5 (Total Absorption):** Quality: Tactile reality. The experience is intensely physical, seemingly allowing the viewer to feel the air, temperature, and texture of the environment. The sensory envelopment is complete.  
* **B. Psychological & Emotional Verisimilitude (PEV):**  
  * **L1 (Detached):** Quality: Artificial and unbelievable. Performances are exaggerated or stiff. Emotional arcs are contrived (manipulation), preventing empathy. Characters are used as tools.  
  * **L2 (Conventional):** Quality: Plausible performances. Character motivations and emotions are understandable. Elicits appropriate emotional responses, but lacks depth or profound connection.  
  * **L3 (Immersive):** Quality: Authentic performances that feel like reality. The complex inner lives of the characters are conveyed with depth, causing strong emotional engagement. Even stylized performances feel true within the film's world.  
  * **L4 (Captivating):** Quality: Deep internalization. The boundary between actor and character dissolves. The viewer deeply internalizes the character's pain, joy, or anxiety as their own emotion.  
  * **L5 (Total Absorption):** Quality: Psychological melding. The viewer experiences a complete identification with the character's psyche, resulting in an indelible emotional impact and profound psychological realism.  
* **C. Diegetic Coherence (DC):**  
  * **L1 (Detached):** Quality: Unstable and inconsistent. The rules of the world are inconsistent. Plot holes, logical fallacies, or setting errors constantly break immersion. The "suspension of disbelief" fails.  
  * **L2 (Conventional):** Quality: Stable world-building. Maintains a consistent worldview. Minor inconsistencies may exist but do not ruin the experience. Relies on established genre logic.  
  * **L3 (Immersive):** Quality: Robust construction. An original worldview is intricately designed. Even if unrealistic or stylized, the internal logic is sound and persuasive.  
  * **L4 (Captivating):** Quality: Absolute internal logic. The world operates with ironclad consistency. Even absurd or surreal worlds feel completely autonomous, making the audience accept the rules without question.  
  * **L5 (Total Absorption):** Quality: Ontological reality. The constructed world achieves a level of coherence so profound that it feels like an alternate reality, challenging the viewer's perception of their own world.  
* **D. Attentional Command (AC):**  
  * **L1 (Detached):** Quality: Scattered attention. The flow is frequently interrupted. Boring or distracting direction prevents sustained immersion. The medium itself (editing, camera work) is intrusive and jarring.  
  * **L2 (Conventional):** Quality: Engaging narrative. Generally interesting progression. There are compelling scenes, but the intensity of immersion is uneven. The viewer remains aware of the outside world.  
  * **L3 (Immersive):** Quality: Compelling rhythm. The film's rhythm controls the viewer's breathing. Editing rhythms or long takes create tension or contemplative immersion, making the medium feel transparent.  
  * **L4 (Captivating):** Quality: Powerful grip. The film completely dominates the viewer's consciousness from beginning to end. A powerful sense of flow is maintained throughout the duration.  
  * **L5 (Total Absorption):** Quality: Hypnotic state. Induces a trance-like state where time perception is distorted and self-awareness is lost. The viewer is completely absorbed in the filmic experience.

**II. Difficulty/Contextuality Index Framework**

* **A. Intertextuality (ITX):**  
  * L1 (Foundational): Requirement: No prior knowledge of film history or conventions is needed. The film functions independently.  
  * L2 (Intermediate): Requirement: Familiarity with basic genre conventions (e.g., Western, Noir) enhances appreciation, as the film utilizes these tropes effectively.  
  * L3 (Advanced): Requirement: Significant knowledge of film history or specific genres is required. The film frequently engages in homage, parody, or references major film movements (e.g., French New Wave).  
  * L4 (Expert): Requirement: Deep understanding of film history is essential. The film critically engages with, deconstructs, or subverts past aesthetics and genre conventions.  
  * L5 (Mastery): Requirement: Encyclopedic knowledge of film history and theory is mandatory. The film is meta-cinematic, exploring the nature of the medium itself.  
* **B. Formal Radicalism (FR):**  
  * L1 (Foundational): Requirement: Adheres to classical style: continuity editing, linear narrative, clear causality, and conventional pacing. Highly accessible.  
  * L2 (Intermediate): Requirement: Primarily conventional but incorporates stylized elements (unique mise-en-scène, distinct editing rhythms) within an accessible narrative framework.  
  * L3 (Advanced): Requirement: Challenges conventions: Non-linear narratives, ambiguous causality, and unconventional techniques (jump cuts, long takes). Requires active interpretation and familiarity with modernist cinema.  
  * L4 (Expert): Requirement: Experimental forms: Rejects narrative pleasure, emphasizes contemplation (Slow Cinema). Dream/reality boundaries are blurred. Significant patience and experience are required.  
  * L5 (Mastery): Requirement: Avant-garde or extreme forms: Anti-narrative, structuralist film. Tests physical endurance (extreme long takes, duration) or emotional endurance (Transgressive Cinema).  
* **C. Extratextuality (ETX):**  
  * L1 (Foundational): Requirement: Addresses universal themes (love, family, conflict). No background knowledge required.  
  * L2 (Intermediate): Requirement: Utilizes widely known historical or cultural backgrounds (e.g., WWII, the American Dream). General knowledge is sufficient.  
  * L3 (Advanced): Requirement: Understanding specific national or historical contexts (e.g., Cold War politics, specific revolutions) is crucial for grasping the core narrative and themes.  
  * L4 (Expert): Requirement: Delves into obscure history, complex regional dilemmas, or specific philosophical concepts (e.g., Existentialism). Significant prior research is necessary.  
  * L5 (Mastery): Requirement: Based on specialized philosophy, ideology, or aesthetic theory. Knowledge of the specific field is mandatory for access.  
* **D. Intratextuality (CTX):**  
  * L1 (Foundational): Requirement: Can be fully appreciated without knowledge of the director's other works. A standalone piece.  
  * L2 (Intermediate): Requirement: The director's style is recognizable, but prior films are not necessary for comprehension.  
  * L3 (Advanced): Requirement: The director employs clear recurring motifs or thematic concerns. Knowledge of previous works significantly enriches the interpretation.  
  * L4 (Expert): Requirement: The film deepens or complicates themes from previous works. A deep understanding of the director's established world and stylistic language is crucial.  
  * L5 (Mastery): Requirement: The director's aesthetic/philosophical journey itself is the core subject. Sequential understanding from early works is mandatory to grasp the evolution and significance.

**III. Possibility of Disappointment Index Framework**

* **A. Intellectual Bankruptcy:**  
  * **L1 (Zero Bankruptcy):** Definition: The narrative is logically sound, structurally robust, and provides satisfying intellectual engagement. Complexity is matched by internal logic.  
  * **L2 (No Bankruptcy):** Definition: The plot is somewhat predictable, or features minor logical leaps and conveniences, but the narrative remains largely coherent.  
  * **L3 (Disappointing):** Definition: (1) Banality: The narrative is excessively clichéd, offering no intellectual stimulation. (2) Confusion: The narrative is unnecessarily convoluted or scattered, making the effort to follow it feel unrewarding and tiresome.  
  * **L4 (Critical Failure):** Definition: (1) Logical Collapse: Severe plot holes destroy narrative credibility. (2) Pretentiousness: The film uses empty symbols and concepts to feign depth, alienating the viewer (pretentious obscurity).  
  * **L5 (Total Bankruptcy):** Definition: The narrative is completely collapsed and incomprehensible, or it demonstrates an extreme level of superficiality and laziness that feels insulting to the viewer's intelligence.  
* **B. Aesthetic Insincerity:**  
  * **L1 (Zero Insincerity):** Definition: All aesthetic elements are masterfully controlled and intentional. Form and content are perfectly integrated, establishing a unique and coherent style.  
  * **L2 (No Insincerity):** Definition: Technically stable but stylistically unremarkable. Some scenes may exhibit weak directorial control or ambiguous aesthetic choices. The execution is functional rather than inspired.  
  * **L3 (Disappointing):** Definition: (1) Incompetence/Mismatch: Awkward execution breaks immersion, or aesthetic choices clash with the theme/tone. (2) Style Over Substance: Ostentatious cinematography or editing is used purely for effect, detached from the subject matter.  
  * **L4 (Critical Failure):** Definition: (1) Aesthetic Inertia (Pastiche): Lacks original style, superficially mimicking other directors or adopting trends. (2) Loss of Control: Technical execution is significantly flawed, elements clash, or the film's tone is wildly inconsistent.  
  * **L5 (Total Bankruptcy):** Definition: Total aesthetic failure. The style is extremely vulgar and crude, or the basic grammar of film language is absent, causing visual/auditory pain. Amateurish execution.  
* **C. Artistic Cowardice:**  
  * **L1 (Zero Cowardice):** Definition: A bold, original vision is pursued without compromise. The film pioneers new artistic territory regardless of commercial considerations, demonstrating profound sincerity.  
  * **L2 (No Cowardice):** Definition: A vision exists but is executed too safely, or the thematic depth remains superficial. The film appears to lack artistic ambition or courage, favoring palatability.  
  * **L3 (Disappointing):** Definition: (1) Complacency/Convention: Relies on genre conventions or trends, diluting originality. Mechanically repeats art-house clichés. (2) Superficiality: Pretends to address profound themes but lacks deep consideration, resulting in emptiness.  
  * **L4 (Critical Failure):** Definition: (1) Commercial Compromise: Artistic vision is subordinate to commercial intent (box office, Oscar bait). (2) Calculated Manipulation/Exploitation: Intent to exploit emotions (melodrama, exploitative violence/poverty) is evident, causing strong resistance.  
  * **L5 (Total Bankruptcy):** Definition: Artistic fraud or ethical bankruptcy. A cynical product devoid of the creator's soul, or an extremely deceptive or unethical attitude that provokes deep disgust and anger.
