The secret bunker beneath the Park house
2 readingsOpen →

In Parasite, a hidden bunker lies beneath the Park family's basement, accessed through a passage behind a shelf. The former housekeeper's husband, Geun-sae, has lived there in hiding for years, sending Morse-code messages with a light switch and praising 'Mr. Park' as his provider.

The Park house vs. the Kim semi-basement
0 readingsOpen →

In Parasite, the Park family occupies a hillside modernist house with a wide lawn and floor-to-ceiling glass, while the Kim family lives in a semi-basement apartment where the toilet sits raised near the ceiling and street-level windows show passing feet. Characters repeatedly climb up or down stairs to move between the two.

Themes & motifs
The tonal shifts — comedy to thriller to tragedy
4 readingsOpen →

Parasite opens as a sly con comedy, pivots into home-invasion thriller when the old housekeeper returns, and ends in bloody tragedy at the garden party. Bong cuts between registers without warning.

In Parasite, Min gifts the Kims a suseok mounted on a wooden stand, calling it metaphorical. Ki-woo carries it through the film, takes it into the flooded basement, and later uses it in the bunker, where Geun-sae strikes him with it.

In Parasite, Mrs. Park phones from the car and asks the housekeeper to prepare ram-don — a dish combining two cheap instant noodle brands — topped with premium Hanwoo sirloin. Chung-sook cooks it in roughly eight minutes while the family hides under the table nearby.
