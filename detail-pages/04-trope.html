A Separation (2011) — the film's generic identity →

A Separation blends courtroom procedure, domestic drama, and moral thriller — building suspense around a disputed miscarriage and a question of who lied, while staying rooted in everyday household conflict.

Anora (2024) — shift from rom-com to violent thriller →

Anora opens with the bright tempo of a romance — meet-cute, whirlwind wedding, Vegas montage — then pivots into a tense, chaotic chase and confinement after the parents intervene. The tone, pacing, and music change registers around the midpoint.

Armageddon (1998) — narrative structure & archetypes →

In Armageddon, the plot assembles a ragtag drilling crew, a NASA mission, a father-daughter conflict, a romance, and a heroic sacrifice, drawing on Western, war, and space-adventure conventions.

Barton Fink (1991) — overall narrative & aesthetic →

Barton Fink shifts registers without warning, moving from a 1941 Hollywood satire to a hotel-bound psychological dread to a fiery near-supernatural climax, ending on an unexplained beach image that mirrors a picture in Barton's room.

Chungking Express (1994) — crime/noir vs. rom-com structure →

Chungking Express tells two loosely linked stories in sequence: the first follows a heartbroken cop and a drug-running woman in a blonde wig; the second follows a different cop and the snack-bar worker who breaks into his flat.

Django Unchained (2012) — the anachronistic hip-hop / R&B score →

Django Unchained scores its 1858 setting with anachronistic music, dropping modern hip-hop and R&B tracks by artists like Rick Ross and 2Pac alongside Morricone and period-style cues.

Donnie Darko (2001) — overall tone & structure →

Donnie Darko unfolds across 28 days counting down to a predicted apocalypse, shifting between high-school comedy, suburban family drama, sci-fi physics, and horror. Title cards mark dates; the narrative loops back to its own opening.

Ghost (1990) — generic hybridity →

Ghost shifts between romance, supernatural fantasy, comedy, and thriller, opening as a love story, becoming a murder mystery, running a heist, and closing on tearful farewell.

Gremlins (1984) — horror / comedy / Christmas mix →

Gremlins shifts between gentle small-town Christmas comedy, splatstick creature carnage, and outright horror, often within a single scene — a kitchen battle plays for laughs and gore at once.

Law of Desire (1987) — blending of high & low-culture genres →

Law of Desire shifts between Cocteau-derived theatrical staging, thriller mechanics, melodrama, and bawdy comedy within single sequences, mixing literary allusion with pulp.

Memories of Matsuko (2006) — visual style: editing, genre blend, CGI →

Memories of Matsuko mixes rapid cuts, animation inserts, musical numbers, melodrama, and digitally saturated color, shifting registers and genres scene to scene throughout.

Parasite (2019) — tonal shifts: comedy → thriller → tragedy →

Parasite opens as a sly con comedy, pivots into home-invasion thriller when the old housekeeper returns, and ends in bloody tragedy at the garden party. Bong cuts between registers without warning.

Raising Arizona (1987) — overall generic composition →

Raising Arizona blends fast-talking screwball comedy, crime-caper plotting, biblical narration, western desert iconography, and cartoon-style chase action into a single tonally unstable whole.

Sorry to Bother You (2018) — aesthetic & narrative structure →

Sorry to Bother You shifts from grounded workplace comedy into escalating surrealism — sight gags, abrupt tonal swerves, and a body-horror climax that breaks realism entirely.

Swimming Pool (2003) — narrative structure & thematic elements →

Swimming Pool drifts between writer's-block character study, erotic thriller, and possible murder mystery, never settling — a corpse may be buried in the garden, yet the film withholds confirmation and dissolves the plot into Sarah's manuscript.

The Fifth Element (1997) — narrative & generic hybridity →

In The Fifth Element, an ancient-Egypt prologue gives way to space opera, a screwball cab-driver romance, a comic radio-host subplot, gun-battle action, and a cosmic countdown to save Earth, all within one continuous story.

The Lives of Others (2006) — overall narrative structure →

The Lives of Others moves from a procedural surveillance thriller — bugging, transcripts, interrogation — into a chamber melodrama of conscience and love, then a post-1989 coda spanning years, closing on a bookshop in reunified Berlin.

The Wailing (2016) — overall narrative structure →

The Wailing shifts across police procedural, infection thriller, family melodrama, occult horror, and folk mystery, withholding any final explanation of who is good, who is evil, or what truly caused the deaths.
