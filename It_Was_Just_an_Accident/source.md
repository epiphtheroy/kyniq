# Source Material — It Was Just an Accident (Jafar Panahi, 2025)

> Compiled from the MetaTake DB (films, film_reception, figures, takes) and verified against web sources (Cannes 2025, cast, plot). This is the source.md for the Anthony Lane essay pipeline.

## Verified facts (web + DB)

- Title: *It Was Just an Accident* (Persian: یک تصادف ساده; French festival title: *Un simple accident*)
- Director/writer: Jafar Panahi (b. 1960)
- Year: 2025. Runtime: 104 min. Genres: Drama / Thriller / Crime / Mystery. Certification US:PG-13. US release ~Oct 1, 2025.
- World premiere: 78th Cannes Film Festival, 20 May 2025 — **won the Palme d'Or**.
- Made clandestinely, without official filming permission from Iranian authorities.
- Marks Panahi's return to pure narrative fiction; first film in years in which he himself does not appear.
- Cast: Vahid Mobasseri (Vahid, the mechanic); Ebrahim Azizi (Eghbal, the suspected interrogator — the **sole professional actor**); Maryam Afshari, Hadis Pakbaten, Majid Panahi, Mohamad Ali Elyasmehr, Georges Hashemzadeh, Delmaz Najafi, Afssaneh Najmabadi.
- Panahi's legal history: 2010 sentence banning him from filmmaking and travel for 20 years; arrested and imprisoned at Evin Prison in 2022 (released 2023). Career began alongside Abbas Kiarostami's screenplays.
- Panahi filmography in DB: The White Balloon (1995), The Circle (2000), Offside (2006), This Is Not a Film (2011, smuggled to Cannes on a USB inside a cake), Taxi (2015), 3 Faces (2018), No Bears (2022), It Was Just an Accident (2025).

## Plot / premise

An unassuming garage mechanic, Vahid, becomes convinced that a customer is "Eghbal," the man who tortured him in an Iranian prison. He recognizes the man not by his face — the prisoners were blindfolded — but by the rhythmic squeak of his prosthetic leg. Vahid abducts the suspect, digs a shallow grave in the desert scrubland outside Tehran, and gathers fellow ex-prisoners (a wedding photographer, a bookseller, and others) to confirm or deny the identity. The suspect's heavily pregnant wife waits, oblivious, in a parked car. The film never visually confirms the suspect's guilt.

## Figures (scenes / motifs / forms catalogued in DB)

- **Vahid the mechanic** — ordinary workday collapses when he believes a customer is his torturer.
- **The pregnant wife waiting in the car** — oblivious to the accusation; her presence complicates any plan to make her husband disappear.
- **The prosthetic leg's creak** — the captor identified by sound, not sight; the blindfolded prisoners memorized the squeak.
- **Panahi's contraband realism** — handheld, vehicle-bound, present-tense style; shot clandestinely by a banned, once-imprisoned director.
- **The shallow grave in the desert** — empty scrubland Vahid digs and returns to repeatedly.
- **The unconfirmed face of the suspect** — guilt kept suspended; torture never shown, identity never verified.
- **The gathering of ex-prisoners** — a wedding photographer, a bookseller, others summoned to confirm identity.
- **The title** — "It Was Just an Accident."

## Interpretive cards (figure × theorist × concept)

- **Title → Hannah Arendt (banality of evil / bureaucratic language rules):** "It was just an accident" is the precise rhetoric by which torturing states euphemize their crimes — prisoners who "fell," who "died of natural causes." The title is the perpetrator's standard alibi; the lie the film exists to dismantle.
- **Unconfirmed face → Bernard Williams (moral luck):** By never confirming identity, the film stages the condition under which retribution becomes intolerable: punishment without proof. The agony of risking the destruction of an innocent to satisfy a certainty one cannot have. Certainty is what vengeance can never possess.
- **The creak → Michel Chion (acousmêtre):** the captor known by sound whose unseen source holds total power precisely because it can't be located. State terror as an acoustic, faceless phenomenon the eye can never confirm. The creak is the noumenon of authoritarian power.
- **Pregnant wife → Emmanuel Levinas (the face of the Other / infinite responsibility):** to kill the suspect is to orphan an innocent and punish a future that committed no crime. Every act of vengeance creates new victims, replicating the regime's logic. The unborn child is the moral hostage that turns justice into atrocity — completing vengeance would make the avengers indistinguishable from their torturers.
- **Contraband realism → context:** handheld, car-bound form is the direct fingerprint of the 2010 filmmaking ban and the 2022 Evin imprisonment. The car becomes the studio because the street is surveilled. The "style" is the visible scar of state persecution.
- **The grave → 1988 prison massacres / mass graves allegory:** the unmarked pits of Iran's disappeared; a society circling buried crimes it can neither fill nor leave.
- **Vahid → Freud (repetition compulsion):** the mechanic fixes broken machines but cannot repair the unrepairable wound; trauma returns as a sound. He repairs cars because he cannot repair himself.
- **The film as a whole → Walter Benjamin (Critique of Violence, law-making vs law-preserving):** a tribunal with no legitimate authority to convene it; justice privatized; the collapse of the state's monopoly on justice, forcing citizens to improvise law in a parked van and a desert pit.
- **Gathering of ex-prisoners → transitional justice / the people's tribunal:** who holds the authority to judge agents of state violence when the state shields them. A truth-and-reconciliation commission from below.
- **Contraband realism → Edward Said (Orientalism) [metacritic]:** the festival circuit's framing of the film as heroic dissident testimony reveals Western liberal appetites; the Palme d'Or as Western absolution, consuming Iranian suffering as redemptive spectacle.
- **Juxtaposition → Vedran Smailović, the cellist of Sarajevo (b. 1956):** played Albinoni's Adagio amid the rubble for 22 days. Both ordinary men who refuse the logic of retaliation; civilization survives only in the refusal to answer barbarism in kind.
