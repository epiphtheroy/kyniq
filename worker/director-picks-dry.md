# Where to Start — DRY (6) · claude-opus-4-8

## Bong Joon Ho  ·  6/6 films routed
1. **Start here** — *The Host* (2006)
   The Host is Bong's most accessible blend of monster-movie thrills and family farce, smuggling sharp anti-authoritarian satire into a creature feature that newcomers can enjoy on pure entertainment terms.
2. **The peak** — *Parasite* (2019)
   Parasite is the summit: a precision-engineered class fable that slides from comedy to dread, where Bong's tonal control and architectural staging — the rich house, the basement — earn every shock.
3. **The turn** — *Memories of Murder* (2003)
   Memories of Murder reveals Bong as a serious craftsman, turning a real serial-killer case into a study of incompetence and despair, with the muddy rural procedural style that defines his early work.
4. **If you loved that** — *Snowpiercer* (2013)
   Snowpiercer takes Bong's class warfare onto a literal moving train, each carriage a new world, his first English-language swing at blockbuster scale without losing political bite.
5. **The wild card** — *Okja* (2017)
   Okja pivots to a girl-and-her-creature adventure that mutates into corporate-meat satire, showing Bong's tonal whiplash and tenderness in equal measure across genres.
6. **Deep cut** — *Barking Dogs Never Bite* (2000)
   Barking Dogs Never Bite is the rarely-seen debut: a darkly comic apartment-complex tale of frustration and missing dogs that already shows Bong's eye for cramped social spaces.

## Park Chan-wook  ·  8/8 films routed
1. **Start here** — *The Handmaiden* (2016)
   The Handmaiden is Park's most seductive entry point: a triple-crossing erotic thriller whose lush production design and gleeful structural reversals introduce his obsessions without the brutal cost of the vengeance films.
2. **The peak** — *Oldboy* (2003)
   Oldboy is the summit — a hammer-swinging, single-take corridor fight and a labyrinth of guilt that shows why Park's cocktail of formal precision and emotional savagery made his name.
3. **The turn** — *Decision to Leave* (2022)
   Decision to Leave reveals the mature Park: a restrained, melancholy detective romance built on surveillance and longing, trading gore for aching control and the most delicate camerawork of his career.
4. **Deep cut** — *Sympathy for Mr. Vengeance* (2002)
   Sympathy for Mr. Vengeance is the cold, overlooked first chapter of the revenge trilogy — quieter and bleaker than Oldboy, built on deafness, kidnapping and spiraling working-class despair.
5. **If you loved Oldboy** — *Lady Vengeance* (2005)
   Lady Vengeance completes the trilogy through a woman's meticulously plotted reckoning, its candy-colored stylization curdling into a startling meditation on collective guilt and atonement.
6. **The wild card** — *Thirst* (2009)
   Thirst recasts vampire mythology through a priest's crisis of faith and desire — gory, funny and genuinely strange, showing Park's appetite for genre he can twist into moral horror.
7. **Where it began** — *Joint Security Area* (2000)
   Joint Security Area is the breakthrough that launched him: a tense DMZ murder investigation whose unexpected cross-border friendship proves Park could wring deep humanism from a thriller's machinery.
8. **English detour** — *Stoker* (2013)
   Stoker is Park's icy English-language debut, a coming-of-age gothic dripping with menace and immaculate match-cuts — the clearest view of his eye divorced from Korean context.

## Wong Kar-Wai  ·  8/8 films routed
1. **Start here** — *Chungking Express* (1994)
   Chungking Express is Wong's most buoyant, accessible entry: two lovelorn cops, a pineapple obsession, and 'California Dreamin' on loop. It distills his romantic restlessness into pure, playful energy.
2. **The peak** — *In the Mood for Love* (2000)
   In the Mood for Love is his summit of longing — two neighbors circling an unspoken affair in cheongsams and slow-motion noodle runs. Christopher Doyle's color and Shigeru Umebayashi's waltz define the Wong style.
3. **If you loved that** — *Happy Together* (1997)
   Happy Together follows a Hong Kong couple unraveling in Buenos Aires, swinging from saturated color to harsh monochrome. It's his rawest, most physical portrait of love that can't quit itself.
4. **Where it began** — *Days of Being Wild* (1990)
   Days of Being Wild is the true origin of Wong's mood cinema: a 1960s Hong Kong of clocks, regret, and a 'legless bird.' It launches the universe In the Mood and 2046 complete.
5. **The continuation** — *2046* (2004)
   2046 picks up Tony Leung's writer haunted by past loves, blending sci-fi reverie with hotel-room melancholy. It rewards anyone who's absorbed Days of Being Wild and In the Mood for Love.
6. **The wild card** — *Fallen Angels* (1995)
   Fallen Angels is Chungking Express's nocturnal twin — a hitman, his agent, and a mute drifter shot in dizzying wide-angle neon. Pure kinetic delirium for the now-hooked viewer.
7. **Deep cut** — *Ashes of Time* (1994)
   Ashes of Time bends the wuxia epic into fragmented, sun-blasted meditation on memory and loss. Its swordsmen barely fight; they brood — the least conventional, most demanding film here.
8. **Where it all started** — *As Tears Go By* (1988)
   As Tears Go By is Wong's debut, a Triad gangster romance still tied to genre convention yet flashing his future lyricism. The seed of everything that follows.

## Christopher Nolan  ·  8/12 films routed
1. **Start here** — *Inception* (2010)
   Inception is Nolan's most accessible thesis statement: a heist movie nested inside dreams that turns exposition into spectacle, teaching newcomers his rules of layered time and architecture before the harder stuff.
2. **If you loved that** — *The Dark Knight* (2008)
   The Dark Knight grounds the scale in a real city and a moral ledger, with Heath Ledger's Joker forcing escalating ethical choices that prove Nolan can make a blockbuster genuinely unsettling.
3. **Where it began** — *Memento* (2000)
   Memento runs its revenge story backward through a man who can't form memories, the formal puzzle-box experiment that established Nolan's obsession with fractured chronology and unreliable knowledge.
4. **The peak** — *Oppenheimer* (2023)
   Oppenheimer fuses Nolan's time-jumping structure with intimate close-ups and a ticking conscience, building the bomb test as pure dread—his most mature marriage of formal ambition and human stakes.
5. **The deep cut** — *The Prestige* (2006)
   The Prestige pits two obsessive magicians against each other, a structurally devious story about deception and sacrifice that rewards the hooked viewer who now trusts Nolan to misdirect.
6. **The turn** — *Dunkirk* (2017)
   Dunkirk strips away dialogue and backstory for three interlocking timelines on land, sea, and air—Nolan as pure sensory engineer, proving suspense needs no exposition.
7. **The origin** — *Following* (1999)
   Following is Nolan's black-and-white debut, shot on weekends for almost nothing: a scrambled noir about a writer shadowing strangers that contains every later obsession in embryo.
8. **The wild card** — *Interstellar* (2014)
   Interstellar pushes Nolan toward open emotion and cosmic scale, where relativity bends time across galaxies and a father's love becomes the equation—his most sentimental swing.

## Hayao Miyazaki  ·  9/11 films routed
1. **Start here** — *My Neighbor Totoro* (1988)
   Totoro distills Miyazaki's essence at its gentlest: childhood wonder, rural Japan, and a wordless faith in nature. The plotless, mood-driven structure is the warmest possible introduction for any age.
2. **If you loved that** — *Kiki's Delivery Service* (1989)
   Kiki's Delivery Service swaps fantasy spectacle for a coming-of-age story about a young witch finding independence and beating creative burnout. It shows Miyazaki's tender realism alongside flight.
3. **The peak** — *Spirited Away* (2001)
   Spirited Away is the fullest expression of his imagination: a bathhouse for spirits teeming with invention, a brave girl, and dense visual storytelling. The Oscar-winner that proves why he matters.
4. **The turn** — *Princess Mononoke* (1997)
   Princess Mononoke darkens the palette into epic ecological conflict, with no easy villains between forest gods and industry. It reveals his fierce, morally complex side for viewers ready to go deeper.
5. **Where it began** — *Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind* (1984)
   Nausicaä is the blueprint: toxic wilderness, a warrior-princess pacifist, and environmental warning. Watching it after Mononoke shows how early Miyazaki's central themes arrived fully formed.
6. **The wild card** — *Howl's Moving Castle* (2004)
   Howl's Moving Castle is his most romantic and structurally loose film, set amid an anti-war fairy tale of curses and shifting ages. The walking castle is among his most delirious creations.
7. **Deep cut** — *Porco Rosso* (1992)
   Porco Rosso follows a cynical, pig-faced WWI flying ace over the Adriatic. This melancholy, adult passion project about aviation and aging is the connoisseur's reward, often overlooked outside Japan.
8. **The personal one** — *The Wind Rises* (2013)
   The Wind Rises abandons fantasy entirely for a biographical drama about an aircraft engineer torn between beauty and war machinery. His most mature, self-reflective work about the cost of creation.
9. **The farewell** — *The Boy and the Heron* (2023)
   The Boy and the Heron is his late, autobiographical riddle: grief, a grand-uncle's collapsing world, and a meditation on legacy. Save it for last—it rewards everything you've seen before.

## David Fincher  ·  8/12 films routed
1. **Start here** — *Se7en* (1995)
   Se7en is the perfect entry: a rain-soaked serial-killer procedure that crystallizes Fincher's signature dread, meticulous darkness, and dread-soaked rhythm. Welcoming because it's a genre hook done with total control.
2. **If you loved that** — *The Social Network* (2010)
   The Social Network swaps crime for boardrooms, proving Fincher's precision works on dialogue and ambition. Sorkin's rapid script plus Fincher's cold framing make founding Facebook feel like a thriller.
3. **The peak** — *Zodiac* (2007)
   Zodiac is Fincher's summit: an obsessive, fact-stacked investigation where the case eats the investigators alive. Its patience, period detail, and refusal of easy answers show exactly why he matters.
4. **The turn** — *Gone Girl* (2014)
   Gone Girl turns marriage into a media war, sharpening Fincher's clinical eye into pitch-black satire. A twisty, controlled descent that rewards the viewer now attuned to his manipulations.
5. **The wild card** — *Fight Club* (1999)
   Fight Club is Fincher unleashed: anarchic, grimy, formally restless, narrated by a man unraveling. A provocation about consumer rot that pairs his control with chaos.
6. **Deep cut** — *The Game* (1997)
   The Game is the underseen reward: an elaborate paranoid puzzle that toys with one wealthy man and the audience alike. Pure Fincher mechanism, often overlooked between his bigger hits.
7. **Late refinement** — *The Killer* (2023)
   The Killer distills Fincher into a process movie about a methodical assassin whose plans fail. Lean, droll, and obsessive—a master quietly studying his own themes of control.
8. **Where it deepens** — *Mank* (2020)
   Mank is the personal detour: a black-and-white Old Hollywood reverie about who wrote Citizen Kane, made from his late father's script. Fincher's craft turned to film history itself.
